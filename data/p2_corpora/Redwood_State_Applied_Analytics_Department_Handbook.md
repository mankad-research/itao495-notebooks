# Department of Applied Analytics Handbook

*Redwood State University. Academic year 2026-2027. Redwood State University is a fictional institution.*

## 1. Mission and Programs

### 1.1 Mission
The Department of Applied Analytics prepares students to turn data into decisions. We teach statistics, programming, machine learning, and communication through projects with real organizations. The department was founded in 2011 and is housed in Alder Hall.

### 1.2 Programs
The department offers three programs: the Bachelor of Science in Applied Analytics (B.S.), the Master of Science in Applied Analytics (M.S.), and a Graduate Certificate in Data Visualization. The Department Chair, Dr. Helen Park-Whitfield, leads the department.

## 2. Degrees and Credit Requirements

### 2.1 Bachelor of Science
The B.S. requires 120 credit hours. Of these, 45 credits are in the major: 27 credits of core courses and 18 credits of electives. A student needs a cumulative GPA of at least 2.0 and a GPA of at least 2.5 in major courses, and must earn a C or better in every core course. The last 30 credits must be completed at Redwood State.

### 2.2 Master of Science
The M.S. requires 33 credit hours: 18 credits of core courses, 9 credits of electives, and 6 credits of capstone work. Students choose between the non-thesis route (AA 698, Graduate Capstone) and the thesis route (AA 699, Thesis). A full-time student normally finishes in 4 semesters, and all requirements must be completed within 5 years of first enrollment. Up to 6 credits of graduate work from another institution may be transferred if the grade was B or better and the courses were taken within the last 5 years.

### 2.3 Graduate Certificate
The Graduate Certificate in Data Visualization requires 12 credit hours, or four courses. A student admitted to the M.S. within 3 years of finishing the certificate may count up to 9 certificate credits toward the M.S. Transfer credits and certificate credits together may not exceed 12 credits.

### 2.4 Course Numbers
Courses numbered 100 to 499 are undergraduate. Courses numbered 500 to 699 are graduate. A few 400-level courses are cross-listed with 500-level courses, and graduate students in those courses complete extra assignments.

### 2.5 Admission to the M.S.
Applicants to the M.S. need a bachelor's degree with a GPA of at least 3.0. They must have completed an introductory statistics course and one programming course before the first semester. Standardized test scores are not required. Fall applications received by January 15 receive priority consideration, and the Director of Graduate Programs chairs the admissions committee.

## 3. Advising

### 3.1 Undergraduate Advising
Every undergraduate has a professional advisor in the Undergraduate Advising Office (UAO) in Alder Hall, Room 210, led by Ms. Priya Raman. Students must meet with their advisor once every semester. Until the meeting is recorded, an advising hold blocks registration. Appointments are booked in the Navigator system, and walk-in hours are Tuesdays and Thursdays from 1:00 p.m. to 4:00 p.m.

### 3.2 Graduate Advising
Each graduate student is assigned a faculty mentor by the end of the first semester. The Director of Graduate Programs, Dr. Marcus Delgado, approves each student's program of study (sometimes called the degree plan). The program of study form is due by the end of the second semester for full-time students and by the end of the third semester for part-time students.

### 3.3 Changing Majors and Leaves of Absence
A student who wants to change into the B.S. program from another Redwood State major needs a GPA of at least 2.75 and a meeting with the UAO. A graduate student may take a leave of absence of up to 2 semesters by submitting a request to the Director of Graduate Programs; time on leave does not count toward the 5-year limit.

## 4. Course Registration and Waitlists

### 4.1 Registration Windows
Registration opens 6 weeks before the first day of each term. Windows open in stages by credits earned: seniors (90 or more credits) and all graduate students on day 1, juniors (60 to 89 credits) on day 2, sophomores (30 to 59 credits) on day 3, and first-year students (fewer than 30 credits) on day 4.

### 4.2 Waitlists
When a section is full, a student may join its waitlist. An undergraduate section holds up to 15 waitlisted students, and a graduate section holds up to 8. When a seat opens, the first student on the list is emailed an offer that expires after 24 hours. Waitlists close at 5:00 p.m. on the last day to add a course. A waitlist spot is not a guarantee of a seat, and a student cannot be on the waitlist for a section that conflicts in time with another course he or she is taking.

### 4.3 Adding, Dropping, and Credit Loads
A student may add a course through the end of week 1. A course dropped by the end of week 2 does not appear on the transcript. A student may withdraw from a course through the end of week 10, and the course then shows a grade of W. An undergraduate may take at most 18 credits in a semester without approval from the UAO, which can approve up to 21. A graduate student is full-time with 9 credits in the fall or spring and 6 credits in the summer.

## 5. Grading and Probation

### 5.1 Grades
The department uses plus and minus grades. An A is worth 4.0 points, an A- is worth 3.7, and a B+ is worth 3.3. Graduate students must keep a cumulative GPA of at least 3.0. Only graduate courses with a grade of C or better count toward the M.S., and no more than two grades of C+ or lower may be counted.

### 5.2 Academic Probation
An undergraduate whose cumulative GPA falls below 2.0 is placed on academic probation for the next semester. If the GPA is still below 2.0 after two consecutive semesters on probation, the student is reviewed for dismissal. A graduate student whose cumulative GPA falls below 3.0 is placed on probation and has the next 9 credits attempted to bring it back to 3.0. A graduate student who fails to do so is recommended for dismissal by the Director of Graduate Programs.

### 5.3 Incompletes and Appeals
A grade of Incomplete (I) must be finished within 6 weeks after the start of the next semester, or it becomes an F. A student who disagrees with a grade has 20 business days after grades are posted to appeal, first to the instructor and then to the Department Chair. Graduate students appeal to the Director of Graduate Programs.

### 5.4 Repeating Courses
An undergraduate may repeat a course in which he or she earned a grade below C. The new grade replaces the old grade in the GPA, but only for the first 12 credits repeated; after that, both grades are averaged. A course may be taken no more than 3 times in total, counting the first attempt.

## 6. Academic Integrity

### 6.1 Principles and Process
Students must submit only their own work and must cite their sources. An instructor who suspects a violation reports it to the Academic Integrity Officer, Dr. Tomas Reyes, within 10 business days. The instructor meets with the student within 5 business days of the report. The student may request a hearing before the three-member Integrity Panel within 10 business days. A first offense normally earns a zero on the assignment, a second offense normally earns an F in the course, and a severe violation can lead to suspension for one semester.

### 6.2 Use of AI in Coursework
Each course syllabus must state one of three AI levels. Level 0 means no generative AI tools may be used. Level 1 means AI tools may be used for brainstorming and for checking grammar only. Level 2 means AI tools may be used more broadly, but the student must attach a disclosure statement listing the tool, the date used, and the prompts entered. If a syllabus does not state a level, Level 0 applies. Exams are always Level 0. Capstone and thesis work is Level 1 unless the Capstone Coordinator approves Level 2 in writing. AI-detection software alone is never sufficient evidence of a violation. Using AI beyond the permitted level is handled as an academic integrity violation under Section 6.1.

### 6.3 Collaboration
Unless the instructor says otherwise, students may discuss assignments but must write and code their own solutions. Group projects must include a statement of each member's contribution. Sharing solutions with students in a later semester is also a violation.

## 7. Capstone and Internships

### 7.1 Capstone Courses
Undergraduates complete AA 498, a 3-credit capstone taken after earning at least 90 credits and finishing AA 350 and AA 410. Master's students begin AA 698 after finishing the 18 core credits with a GPA of at least 3.0. Teams of four or five students work for 14 weeks on a project for an outside sponsor and present at the Analytics Showcase, held on the first Thursday of May and the first Thursday of December. The Capstone Coordinator, Dr. Imani Okafor, matches teams with sponsors.

### 7.2 Internships
A student may earn credit for an internship through AA 293. One credit requires 60 hours of work, and at most 3 internship credits may count as electives. The student needs approval from the Career Services Liaison, Mr. Kenji Watanabe, before the internship begins. Undergraduates need 45 completed credits and a GPA of at least 2.5. International students who need Curricular Practical Training must have completed one academic year of study and must obtain authorization from the Office of International Programs before starting.

### 7.3 Sponsor Confidentiality
Before the first sponsor meeting, every team member signs a nondisclosure agreement arranged by the Capstone Coordinator. Sponsor data must never be entered into public AI tools.

### 7.4 Capstone Deliverables
Each capstone team submits a written report of at most 30 pages by the Friday before the Analytics Showcase. The sponsor completes an evaluation form that counts for 20 percent of the capstone grade. The department sometimes calls the capstone the culminating project.

## 8. Funding and Assistantships

### 8.1 Graduate Assistantships
M.S. students may apply for teaching or research assistantships. A half-time assistantship (20 hours per week) pays $2,450 per month for 9 months and includes a tuition waiver for up to 9 credits per semester. A quarter-time assistantship (10 hours per week) pays $1,225 per month and includes a waiver of 50 percent of tuition. Assistants must enroll in at least 9 credits in the fall and spring and keep a cumulative GPA of at least 3.0. An assistantship can be held for up to 4 semesters. Applications for fall are due February 1, decisions are sent by March 15, and offers must be accepted or declined by April 15.

### 8.2 Probation and Funding
A graduate assistant who is placed on academic probation keeps the assistantship until the end of that semester and loses it if the GPA has not been restored by then.

### 8.3 Scholarships and Travel Funds
The Redwood Analytics Scholarship awards $2,000 to continuing undergraduates with a GPA of 3.5 or higher and at least 45 credits. Applications are due March 1. Graduate students presenting at a conference can receive up to $750 per academic year in travel funds, requested at least 30 days before travel through the Business Manager, Ms. Dana Kowalczyk.

### 8.4 Summer Funding
Assistantships do not continue automatically in the summer. A student who wants summer support applies to the Director of Graduate Programs by April 1. Summer positions are quarter-time only.

## 9. Deadlines

### 9.1 Key Dates for 2026-2027
Fall classes begin Monday, August 24, 2026, and fall registration opens July 13, 2026. The last day to add a fall course is Friday, August 28, the last day to drop without a W is Friday, September 4, and the last day to withdraw with a W is Friday, October 30. Spring classes begin Monday, January 25, 2027, and spring registration opens December 14, 2026.

### 9.2 Graduation Application Deadlines
Applications for graduation are due October 1 for December graduation, February 15 for May graduation, and June 15 for August graduation. A late application is accepted for up to 14 days after the deadline with a $25 late fee.

## 10. Graduation

### 10.1 Applying and Fees
A student applies for graduation through the Registrar's Office. The graduation fee is $60. The advising office audits the degree plan of each applicant, and the student is told of any missing requirements within 3 weeks of applying.

### 10.2 Honors and Thesis Submission
Undergraduates graduate with honors based on cumulative GPA: cum laude at 3.5, magna cum laude at 3.7, and summa cum laude at 3.9. To be eligible, a student must have completed at least 60 credits at Redwood State. A thesis must be submitted to the Graduate School at least 4 weeks before the end of the term.

### 10.3 Commencement and Diplomas
Commencement ceremonies are held in May and December. August graduates may take part in the December ceremony. Diplomas are mailed 6 to 8 weeks after the degree is conferred.
